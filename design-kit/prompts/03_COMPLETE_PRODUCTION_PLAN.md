## 5. One photographic world

Use soft directional daylight, warm neutral surfaces, honest textures, believable scale and a clear hierarchy of actual products. A restrained cream stone, pale wood or natural linen surface can connect the image family. Keep packaging colour accurate. Use brass as an occasional small environmental detail, not a filter that turns every object gold.

The objects should look selected for everyday life. Avoid a hotel-lobby version of luxury, excessive props, cloudy shadows that hide labels, over-soft focus and elaborate floating-product scenes. Relevant contemporary households in Nepal and India do not need flags, religious symbols or a generic exotic backdrop to feel considered.

Use the same light direction, colour temperature and surface family across H01/H02 and category images. Vary the actual composition rather than applying the same arrangement to every product. Shoot/generate products with readable silhouettes at the size they occupy on screen.

Prefer reference-guided generation and compositing. Do not ask a model to redraw exact packaging text as proof of product identity. When it changes a logo, label, net amount, seal, material or scale, keep the original approved product cut-out and generate only the surrounding scene.
