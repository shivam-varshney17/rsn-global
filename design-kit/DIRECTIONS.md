# Three original evergreen directions

These are proposed RSN ONE designs, not fetched copies of reference sites or measured conversion winners. All three share the same retail proposition and shopping routes. Preview examples contain illustrations and layout-only catalogue records.

## 01 / Everyday, selected — recommended

**Idea:** a knowledgeable shopkeeper with an editorial eye. The first screen explains the goods and membership rule while the image makes the collection desirable.

- Split hero, roughly 45% text / 55% image, with an expressive Fraunces headline and readable Inter supporting text.
- Warm paper as the main surface. Espresso anchors navigation and membership. Brass appears only as a small detail.
- Four compact, image-led categories immediately after the hero. The first useful product selection follows before the brand story.
- Four product cards per desktop row and two per mobile row. Product labels follow actual backend price visibility.
- One substantial membership section, one authentic selection story and one short practical FAQ. No repeated poetry sections or decorative institution dashboard.

**Works best when:** the assortment is curated across several daily-life categories and the brand needs to explain its membership relationship.

**Failure mode:** a beautiful but irrelevant hero, unrecognisable product packaging, tiny information or a sourcing story taking too long to reach the products.

## 02 / The useful collection

**Idea:** give the visitor a confident, efficient route to an everyday product.

- A shorter hero, more compact copy and less surrounding space.
- The image receives more desktop width, while the category/product grid arrives sooner.
- Same warm identity and typography; practical information carries more of the page.

**Works best when:** there is enough real catalogue depth and most traffic is ready to browse.

**Failure mode:** looking like a general marketplace with no discernible curatorial point of view. A dense UI cannot compensate for unavailable merchandise or weak photography.

## 03 / The everyday journal

**Idea:** place a coherent selection in a believable household scene, then let visitors shop it.

- Full-width editorial image on desktop with a restrained cream reading panel.
- Compact categories and orderly product information preserve shopping clarity.
- On mobile, copy and the primary action come first, followed by the image. No mobile copy trapped over a busy background.

**Works best when:** authentic photography can show multiple genuinely available categories in a culturally grounded everyday setting.

**Failure mode:** generic luxury interiors, a beauty-only image implying a beauty-only business, or lifestyle atmosphere hiding the offer.

## The design system

| Element | Decision |
|---|---|
| Cream | #F1E9DA; warm panels and member contrast |
| Espresso | #1F1712; text, primary buttons and membership |
| Brass | #C9A25A; restrained detail, never low-contrast body copy |
| Reading surface | #FAF7F0 |
| Headings | Fraunces, regular, sentence case; selected italic phrases |
| Navigation and product information | Inter, regular/medium |
| Desktop hero headline | Approximately 56–78px, adjusted for available width |
| Mobile hero headline | Approximately 49px; readable proposition and action early |
| Product grids | Four columns desktop, two mobile |
| Spacing rhythm | 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 80px; component styles make deliberate intermediate adjustments |
| Image treatment | Natural product colours, readable packaging, consistent light, stable aspect ratios |
| Motion | Optional subtle image hover; reduced-motion support; no reveal dependency |

Fraunces and Inter are specified by the user's brief. The host should retain its actual loaded fonts. Any preview font files must retain their accompanying licenses and provenance record; they are not a reason to add a second font-loading system to production.

## What changed from the old source direction

The current repository represented a wholesale institution with demo metrics. This handoff speaks to households and makes Global Select the primary shopping destination. It does not use shipping boxes, procurement briefs, GMV dashboards or launch-market roadmaps to explain the retail offer. Supplier and administrative access move to the footer or existing secondary routes.
